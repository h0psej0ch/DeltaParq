\section{Experiments \& Results}


\subsection{Correctness}

Before evaluating the performance, the correctness of reconstructing dataframes was verified. For correctness, the reconstructed 
dataframe must be set-equal to the original dataframe:
\begin{gather*}
    reconstructed = original \\
    \Leftrightarrow\quad reconstructed \subseteq original \;\wedge\; original \subseteq reconstructed \\
    \Leftrightarrow\quad reconstructed \setminus original = \emptyset \;\wedge\; original \setminus reconstructed = \emptyset
\end{gather*}
This maps directly to PySpark's $subtract()$ method, where the differences between dataframes 
are computed. Ensuring the count of the result dataframes is equal to 0, will verify the correctness.
All versions of delta generation in the DeltaParq system passed these tests across both datasets.


\subsection{Strategies}

For the evaluation, three primary strategies have been selected alongside the baseline described in Section~\ref{sec:baseline}. 
This selection is based on the following two competing factors. First, a larger dependency step size, meaning the amount of 
days between a delta and its dependency, will result in more data being changed and thus less storage being saved. 
Second, a higher dependency depth, meaning the amount of steps until a full snapshot is reached, will result in a higher 
retrieval time as more computation needs to be done to apply all the steps. The three selected strategies represent 
different possible combinations of these factors. Furthermore, an additional consideration for choosing a delta strategy
is the result of data corruption. If the base of a delta corrupts, the delta itself will also not be recoverable, 
thus if many days depend on a singular day, the amount of data potentially lost increases. 
A visual overview of the selected diagrams can be found in Appendix~\ref{sec:strat_diag}.

\textbf{Anchor:} The anchor strategy uses a singular full snapshot at the first of the month, all other days are a delta based 
on this full snapshot. This results in a constant dependency depth of 1 for all deltas, but comes with a step size 
that grows linearly for each day of the month. The anchor strategy tests the case of minimal retrieval time loss 
at the cost of more storage usage. Furthermore, only the first day of the month has dependencies, and can thus 
cause a big amount of data loss, all the other days only cause data loss for the day itself.

\textbf{Progressive:} The progressive strategy starts with a full snapshot on the first of the month and consequently builds each delta on the 
previous day as a base. This results in a constant step size of 1 for all deltas, but comes with a dependency depth that 
grows linearly for each day of the month. The progressive strategy tests the case of saving the most amount of storage space
at the cost of higher retrieval times. The progressive strategy also has the highest average amount of data loss
as all remaining days depend on the current day and would thus be lost if a single day corrupts.

\textbf{BST:} The binary search tree (BST) strategy is selected as a middle ground of the anchor and progressive strategies.
When comparing the ratio of the average step size and the average step count, the anchor and progressive strategies 
are at the extremes of minimizing the step count and minimizing the step size, as can be seen in Table~\ref{tab:ratio}. 
To approximate a ratio of 1, a strategy based on a BST is used, where day 1 forms the root node and each node $d$ has 
child nodes $2 \cdot d$ and $2 \cdot d + 1$. The dependency chain for any day of the month can then be obtained by walking 
the tree from the node to the root. For data loss, the BST also sits in the middle of the other two strategies. Nodes 
further down the tree have fewer dependencies and thus only few lost days upon corruption, while nodes higher up the tree 
can cause significant loss of data for a single corruption.

% \input{sections/strategy_diagram.tex}
This however, while getting close to a ratio of 1, can be improved further. A pruned version of the BST is created 
by splitting the BST into 2 parts, day 1 to 16 are all part of the normal tree while day 17 to 31 are part of a BST 
of which the root node is node 16. This optimized BST is the version used in the experiments.

\subsection{Overview}

Table~\ref{tab:overview_li} and Table~\ref{tab:overview_ch} summarize the average storage usage and analysis time for a full month for each tested
strategy, along with the baseline, across both evaluated datasets. This shows that, as expected, the Progressive strategy manages to save the most space
over a full month, on average 49.25\% between the 2 datasets, compared to the baseline, but that the analysis time for the full month increases significantly
by 98.65\%. Furthermore, the Anchor strategy doesn't manage to save much space but also doesn't affect the analysis time much. Finally, the BST fits right
in between saving both a reasonable amount of storage while also taking an average of 34.7\% extra analysis time compared to the baseline.

\subsection{Storage Efficiency}

\hspace{\parindent}\textbf{Liechtenstein (.li):}\input{figures/sizes_li.tex} Figure~\ref{fig:storage_sizes_li} shows the average storage size used for each day in the month for each 
strategy over the $.li$ dataset. The first day immediately shows an interesting result, the only difference between
the baseline and the first day of each strategy is the schema decomposition, storing the volatile and non-volatile 
data separately already increases the storage size significantly. Furthermore, both the Anchor and BST strategies 
use more storage space than the baseline for some days. Almost everything was changed between the day and its base 
causing the delta to be almost the same size, while the schema decomposition would increase the size further than 
the baseline. The BST also shows that it gets a new root at day 17, the storage size than follows the same pattern 
as it had at the start of the month. The Progressive strategy does cut the storage usage almost in half throughout 
the whole month indicating no major changes in the dataset overall.

\textbf{Switzerland (.ch):}\input{figures/sizes_ch.tex} The storage size evaluation of the sampled $.ch$ dataset (Figure~\ref{fig:storage_sizes_ch}) shows similar patterns as the $.li$ dataset.
The extra storage size as a result of the schema decomposition is however less significant. Furthermore, the Anchor and BST strategies 
stay below the baseline indicating that every strategy is able to decrease the storage usage for every day, except the first day of the month,
on the sampled $.ch$ dataset.

\subsection{Analysis Efficiency}

\hspace{\parindent}\textbf{Liechtenstein (.li):}\input{figures/times_li.tex} Figure~\ref{fig:analysis_times_li} shows the average analysis times for each day in the month for each strategy over the $.li$ dataset.
Consistent with the amount of dependencies growing linearly, the analysis time of the progressive strategy also grows linearly. Furthermore,
both the baseline and the Anchor strategy stay almost constant, which is as expected since there is no difference in the operations necessary 
for reconstruction between days. The BST strategy, like in the storage efficiency results, is a middle ground between the Anchor and Progressive 
strategy. Finally, the first day of the month shows a high peak for all strategies, compared to the baseline the analysis takes way longer, 
indicating that the schema decomposition with a full snapshot greatly increases the analysis time.

\textbf{Switzerland (.ch):}\input{figures/times_ch.tex} The analysis time evaluation of the sampled $.ch$ dataset (Figure~\ref{fig:analysis_times_ch}) shows the same characteristics as the $.li$ 
dataset for all strategies. The only significant difference is that the Anchor strategy has taken more analysis time than the baseline, but 
still stays constant.

\subsection{Discussion}

\hspace{\parindent}\textbf{Representativeness:} The difference in the outcomes, in particular that the DeltaParq performs worse relative to the baseline on the $.li$ dataset, indicates that 
the underlying datasets differ in structure. A possible explanation for this is DNS parking. Since Liechtenstein is a microstate with a small 
population, the DNS records could be flooded by companies and institutions from all across the world claiming domains with their company name 
or institution name such that the domain cannot be misused. Since all of these parked domains are not changing, but their security signatures 
have to change, the security signatures are overrepresented in the deltas being created. The security signatures however, are generated such 
that they have no patterns and are thus almost uncompressable for regular algorithms, like gzip, making them even more overrepresented in the 
storage sizes being taken up by the deltas.

\textbf{Elevated Analysis Time on Day 1:} A consistent peak in analysis time is observed for all strategies on the first day of the month. 
On the $.li$ dataset this peak is absent in the baseline, suggesting the schema decomposition and resulting volatile join contribute to the 
elevation, as this is the only difference between the baseline and the DeltaParq versions. On the $.ch$ dataset however, 
the baseline also exhibits a day 1 peak, indicating that the schema decomposition is not the sole contributor to this elevation.
Since the PySpark cache is explicitly cleared before the analysis time evaluation of each day, PySpark caching the results and reusing these 
for the retrieval of the next day can be excluded as a cause. A plausible explanation however, is caching of data and operations at a lower level 
in the pipeline. The storage system may retain data and metadata, such as file listings and partition discovery results, retrieved for the first day 
of the month in its own cache and use it for the subsequent days. Conclusively identifying the cause of the elevation would require isolating 
individual pipeline stages, which was outside the scope of this evaluation.

\textbf{Anchoring Interval:}\label{sec:discussion} The results of the Anchor strategy show the storage usage growing fast over the first few days, this shows that Anchoring every first day 
of the month may not be the most efficient version of the Anchor strategy. For example, Anchoring on a weekly basis would reduce the 
step size to a maximum of 6 days and, based on the graph of the first 7 days of the Anchor strategy, save a significant part of the savings 
that the Anchor strategy offers. A pitfall of introducing a smaller window for the Anchor strategy is that the full decomposed snapshot is 
shown to result in significantly higher analysis times for those specific days, having full decomposed snapshots more often would thus 
result in higher average analysis times.

\textbf{Benchmark Limitations:} The evaluation of analysis times was conducted using the existing computing environment used by the OpenINTEL project. While this reflects a realistic scenario, 
it introduces the limitation of shared resources. Countermeasures were taken to minimize this effect, such as avoiding evaluations during the daily data retrieval 
window and performing multiple runs to reduce the variance, but it is not a sandboxed environment and results may therefore be influenced by interference from 
concurrent tasks being executed, especially interactions with the storage which all go through a single pipeline. 

Furthermore, the evaluation was conducted using a single analysis function based on the data of one day at a time. Longitudinal analysis, where the data of 
multiple days is retrieved and analyzed as a group, was not evaluated. In theory, the total retrieval time for a range of days should be at most 
the sum of the individual day retrieval times, and potentially less. The retrieval of each single day may have overlap in the base dataframes necessary to 
reconstruct the original dataframe with other days that need to be retrieved; retrieving these days only once would thus save time. 
The DeltaParq system already accounts for this as it uses deduplication and a strict order during the dependency resolution required to retrieve all dataframes.
The effects of this have however not been evaluated and could be of interest for the practical applicability of the system.
