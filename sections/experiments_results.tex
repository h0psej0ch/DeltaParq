\section{Experiments \& Results}


\subsection{Correctness}

Before evaluating the performance, the correctness of reconstructing dataframes was verified. For correctness, the reconstructed 
dataframe must be set-equal to the original dataframe:
\begin{gather*}
    reconstructed = original \\
    \Leftrightarrow\quad reconstructed \subseteq original \;\wedge\; original \subseteq reconstructed \\
    \Leftrightarrow\quad reconstructed \setminus original = \emptyset \;\wedge\; original \setminus reconstructed = \emptyset
\end{gather*}
This maps directly to pyspark's $subtract()$ method, where the differences between dataframes 
are computed. Ensuring the count of the result dataframes is equal to 0 will verify the correctness.
All versions of delta generation in the DeltaParq system passed these tests across both datasets.


\subsection{Strategies}

For the evaluation, three primary strategies have been selected alongside the baseline described in Section~\ref{sec:baseline} 
This selection is based on the following two competing factors. First, a larger dependency step size, meaning the amount of 
days between a delta and its dependency, will result in more data being changed and thus less storage being saved. 
Second, a higher dependency depth, meaning the amount of steps until a full snapshot is reached, will result in a higher 
retrieval time as more computation needs to be done to apply all the steps. The three selected strategies represent 
different possible combinations of these factors.

\subsubsection{Anchor}

The anchor strategy uses a singular full snapshot at the first of the month, all other days are a delta based 
on this full snapshot. This results in a constant dependency depth of 1 for all deltas, but comes with a step size 
that grows linearly for each day of the month. The anchor strategy tests the case of minimal retrieval time loss 
at the cost of more storage usage.

\subsubsection{Progressive}

The progressive strategy starts with a full snapshot on the first of the month and consequently builds each delta on the 
previous day as a base. This results in a constant step size of 1 for all deltas, but comes with a dependency depth that 
grows linearly for each day of the month. The progressive strategy tests the case of saving the most amount of storage space
at the cost of higher retrieval times.

\subsubsection{BST}

The binary search tree (BST) strategy is selected as a middle ground of the anchor and progressive strategies.
When comparing the ratio of the average step size and the average step count, the anchor and progressive strategies 
are at the extremes of minimizing the step count and minimizing the step size, as can be seen in Table~\ref{tab:ratio}. 
To approximate a ratio of 1, a strategy based on a BST is used, where day 1 forms the root node and each node $d$ has 
child nodes $2 \cdot d$ and $2 \cdot d + 1$. The dependency chain for any day of the month can then be obtained by walking 
the tree from the node to the root. 

% \input{sections/strategy_diagram.tex}
This however, while getting close to a ratio of 1, can be improved further. A pruned version of the BST is created 
by splitting the BST into 2 parts, day 1 to 16 are all part of the normal tree while day 17 to 31 are part of a BST 
of which the root node is node 16%, as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:strategies}
. This optimized BST is the version used in the experiments.

\subsection{Storage Efficiency}

\subsubsection{Liechtenstein (.li)}
\input{figures/sizes_li.tex}
Figure~\ref{fig:storage_sizes_li} shows the average storage size used for each day in the month for each 
strategy over the $.li$ dataset. The first day immediately shows an interesting result, the only difference between
the baseline and the first day of each strategy is the schema decomposition, storing the volatile and non-volatile 
data separately already increases the storage size significantly. Furthermore, both the Anchor and BST strategies 
use more storage space than the baseline for some days. Almost everything was changed between the day and its base 
causing the delta to be almost the same size, while the schema decomposition would increase the size further than 
the baseline. The BST also shows that it gets a new root at day 17, the storage size than follows the same pattern 
as it had at the start of the month. The Progressive strategy does cut the storage usage almost in half throughout 
the whole month indicating no major changes in the dataset overall.

\subsubsection{Switzerland (.ch)}
\input{figures/sizes_ch.tex}
The storage size evaluation of the sampled $.ch$ dataset (Figure~\ref{fig:storage_sizes_ch}) shows similar patterns as the $.li$ dataset.
The extra storage size as a result of the schema decomposition is however less significant. Furthermore, the Anchor and BST strategies 
stay below the baseline indicating that every strategy is able to decrease the storage usage for every day, except the first day of the month,
on the sampled $.ch$ dataset.

\input{figures/times_li.tex}

\subsection{Discussion}

\subsubsection{Representativeness}

The difference in the outcomes, in particular that the DeltaParq performs worse relative to the baseline on the $.li$ dataset, indicates that 
the underlying datasets differ in structure. A possible explanation for this is DNS parking. Since Liechtenstein is a microstate with a small 
population, the DNS records could be flooded by companies and institutions from all across the world claiming domains with their company name 
or institution name such that the domain cannot be misused. Since all of these parked domains are not changing, but their security signatures 
have to change, the security signatures are overrepresented in the deltas being created. The security signatures however, are generated such 
that they have no patterns and are thus almost uncompressable for regular algorithms, like gzip, making them even more overrepresented in the 
storage sizes being taken up by the deltas.

\subsubsection{Elevated Analysis Time on Day 1}

A consistent peak in analysis time is observed for all strategies on the first day of the month. While present in the sampled $.ch$ dataset
the baseline does show a similar peak in the $.li$ dataset. Since the only difference between the baseline and the strategies on the first 
day is the schema decomposition, this may contribute to the higher analysis time, as more data needs to be retrieved, and the data needs 
to be joined. This however, does not explain why this is not the case for the sampled $.ch$ dataset where all strategies and the baseline 
have a peak on the first day. Even more so, all other days depend on the first day so the retrieval time of the data from the first day would need to be 
included in all other days as well. The main hypothesis proposed for this result is a cache somewhere in the pipeline. Between the analysis of
each day the pyspark cache is full cleared to ensure pyspark does not use any already retrieved data for the analysis. For the rest of the 
pipeline there is no way to guarantee that no cache is being used, a cache in the S3 storage, Hadoop File System or anywhere in the pipeline 
could cause the result of the first day to be loaded faster when it is needed after already being loaded once. This could further be true 
for other actions in the file system, such as retrieving all files necessary to collect which may be cached by the file system.

\subsubsection{Anchoring Interval}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results of the Anchor strategy show the storage usage growing fast over the first few days, this shows that Anchoring every first day 
of the month may not be the most efficient version of the Anchor strategy. For example, Anchoring on a weekly basis would reduce the 
step size to a maximum of 6 days and, based on the graph of the first 7 days of the Anchor strategy, save a significant part of the savings 
that the Anchor strategy offers. A pitfall of introducing a smaller window for the Anchor strategy is that the full decomposed snapshot is 
shown to result in significantly higher analysis times for those specific days, having full decomposed snapshots more often would thus 
result in higher average analysis times.
